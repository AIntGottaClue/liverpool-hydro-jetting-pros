Frequently Asked Questions About Hydro Jetting in Second Street museum area

Does an 1857 landmark nearby tell me anything about my pipes?

No. Local history does not identify private pipe material, age or condition. Records and an inspection do.

Is high pressure safe for older lines?

It depends on the line's condition. A sound one can take it, and a weak one may need repair first.

What should I tell the crew about my house?

Share its approximate age, any plumbing repairs you know of, and which fixtures are slow.

Can roots be the cause in an older village area?

Yes. Mature trees and aged joints are a common combination, and the camera can confirm it.

Does a cleaning fix a cracked pipe?

No. It removes an obstruction. A crack needs a repair assessment.

What details should I give when I request service?

List the affected fixtures, when the problem started, and anything that changed around that time. Mention any past cleanings or repairs, and where the cleanout is if you know.

How is jetting different from snaking?

A snake opens a path through a blockage, while jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water. For residue that keeps causing repeat clogs, jetting addresses what snaking leaves behind, when the pipe's condition allows.

Do I need an inspection before jetting?

Yes. The cause of the blockage decides the method, and a cracked or weak pipe can be made worse by high pressure. Inspection first is the rule for any property.
